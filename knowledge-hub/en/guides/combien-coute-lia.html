How much does AI cost: subscriptions, API, and a bill under control

Everything is counted in tokens. The ways to pay for AI (free, subscription, pay-as-you-go API, enterprise), which one to pick depending on your use, the September 2026 orders of magnitude, and how to keep the bill under control.

By Hugo Lahutte · May 31, 2026 · Updated September 1, 2026 · ~6 min read

30-second take

  • Everything is counted in tokens (bits of words): the longer the exchange, the more it costs; a powerful model costs more than a fast one.
  • Four ways to pay: free (limited), a personal subscription (fixed plan — Pro around $20/month, Max at $100 or $200 for more volume), pay-as-you-go API (you pay for what you consume), enterprise (per user).
  • For one person: the subscription is often enough — and it now includes Chat, Cowork, and Code. To automate and plug in tools: the API, with caps.

1. What drives the cost: tokens

AI doesn't bill "per question." Everything is measured in tokens — fragments of words the model reads (your instructions, the documents you give it, the conversation history) and produces (its answer). The longer the exchange, the more it consumes, so the more it costs. Pasting an 80-page PDF "costs" more than a one-line question — and a three-hour conversation rereads its entire history with every answer (that's the whole point of context).

Second lever: the choice of model. A powerful model (Opus or Fable type) costs more per token than a fast one (Haiku type). Hence the point of picking the right tool for the right task — not the biggest hammer to push in a thumbtack.

2. The ways to pay

Four main models, from the simplest to the most professional. Amounts change fast: think in orders of magnitude, not in prices carved in stone.

3. The orders of magnitude in September 2026

A dated reference point, to get your bearings — the prices below are those of the Claude API, per million tokens, in / out:

  • $1 / $5Haiku 4.5 — the fast one, for volume
  • $2 / $10Sonnet 5 — the balanced one, price made permanent in August
  • $5 / $25Opus 5 — the powerhouse, close to Fable for half the price
  • $10 / $50Fable 5.1 — the class above, to be saved for hard problems

Three things to take away from this table:

  • The price per task matters more than the price per token. A pricier model that solves it in one round trip beats a "cheap" model that needs four. And recent models reuse what they've already read (the cache) at a fraction of the price: a well-structured conversation costs less than a messy one.
  • A Sonnet 5 does today what an Opus did in the spring, five times cheaper. It's the one running my agents.
  • Not every plan gives unlimited access to the most high-end model: the most powerful one is included in the higher plans with a reduced quota, and billed separately on the others. That changed several times this summer; check the current grid rather than taking this guide's word for it.

4. Subscription or API: which one

That's the real practical question, and it's settled by what you do:

In my setup: this site, my Odoo modules, my internal apps, HF-OS — everything is built through the subscription, so at zero marginal cost. What runs without me (the daily briefs, the invoice agent, the review of development requests, Growth) goes through the API and adds up to a few euros a month per automation.

5. Keeping the bill under control

The API is scary ("what if it runs away?"), but it's very easy to frame:

  • Set spending caps. Most API platforms let you set a hard monthly limit. Set it from day one.
  • Pick the right model — and let it pick itself. A fast model for volume and simple tasks; the powerful one only when the difficulty justifies it. And never hard-code a model name: my hi-fi brief stopped for three weeks in June because the model it was calling had reached end of life. Since then, it picks its model automatically and can no longer break on an update.
  • Frame your agents. Budget, frequency, stop criterion — otherwise an agent can loop and burn tokens for nothing (the four rules are in AI proposes, the human validates).
  • Structure the context. One conversation per topic, documents loaded on demand, a handoff brief when it drags on: it's good for quality and for the bill.

For a reference point: my daily hi-fi brief reads 64 sources, writes and publishes every morning, and costs me three to four euros a month — because it's framed and capped. An AI review of a development request, with verification in the real database, costs a few cents. Cost is only a problem when you don't frame it.
