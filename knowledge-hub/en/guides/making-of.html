Making-of:
shipped in a weekend

This site — and an AI Watch bot — from a dinner with friends to the "beautiful baby delivered in 2 days." Without being a developer, paired with Claude Code.

By Hugo Lahutte · June 1, 2026·Updated September 1, 2026 · ~3 min read
build in publicClaude Codecoulisses

The story, short version

Friday, May 29, 2026, dinner with friends. I'm talking again about what I tinker with using AI. Since I'd already told the same story at lunch, I tell myself: I need one single place to show all of this, instead of re-explaining it every time.

Saturday morning, I launch the site. And one thing leads to another, everything follows and goes to production almost immediately:

  • a journal, a glossary, a tag system, a search;
  • a clean dark mode (across 72 pages);
  • an AI Watch page;
  • and even a bot that fetches AI news, has the Claude API summarize it, and publishes it to Telegram + to the site.

The whole thing designed as one piece, right down to the home-made favicon / logo (inspired by Claude, integrated into my art direction) for complete visual consistency.

A beautiful baby delivered in ~2 days — without being a developer, paired with Claude Code.

The two use cases to follow

This is what I document in detail, with figures for time and cost:

  1. The Knowledge Hub site — a static HTML/CSS/JS site, zero framework, on GitHub Pages. Token cost: zero (Claude subscription). Here we're talking time.
  2. The AI Watch bot — a serverless agent (GitHub Actions): X/Twitter → Claude API → Telegram + site. Real cost: a few cents/day (€100 prepaid "to see," it lasts a very long time).

And after that

LinkedIn announcement of the project, documentation of the Telegram channel, and public follow-up of both projects over time — because the best proof that a non-dev can ship is to show the making-of, with the numbers to back it up.

Update · September 1, 2026

And since then

The "beautiful baby delivered in two days" has held up: bilingual in June, reviewed by Fable 5 the day after its release, a hundred pages per language by September, and a complete second pass in September 2026 with Fable 5.1 — method rewritten, six guides added, glossary doubled — because in five months, the ways of working had changed. The principle hasn't moved: document in public, with the numbers to back it up.
