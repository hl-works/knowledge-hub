A competitive-pricing dashboard,
built in-house

The data was already there — competitor prices, collected through Wiser and integrated by our service provider, were running in production. What was missing was the steering layer to do something with it: a spec, quoted at several weeks, left waiting. We built it in-house with Claude Code, in a few days. The most direct build vs buy case of the year.

1. The need: steering your prices against the competition

In specialty retail, price is a permanent battlefield. Knowing, product by product, where you stand against competitors — cheaper, aligned, more expensive — isn't a comfort: it's the basis for deciding where to defend your margin and where to align so you don't lose the sale. But that information has to be readable and actionable, not drowned in an unreadable export you open once a quarter.

At Cobra, the raw material already existed. What was missing was the tool to look at it and act on it.

2. The data already existed — delivered by the integrator

The most technical building block — the import and matching of competitor prices through the Wiser tool (a pricing-intelligence platform that goes and collects the prices charged elsewhere and matches them to our catalog) — had already been delivered by our external integrator (Irokoo) and was running in production. Competitor prices were indeed landing in the system, attached to the right products.

In other words: the “data” part of the problem was solved, and solved well. The hole was elsewhere — in the layer above the data.

3. The quote left waiting

A spec had in fact been sent to the integrator on March 19, 2026 for that missing layer: a price-tracking dashboard (ABC scoring of products, an alignment rules engine, price history), estimated at several weeks of development. That spec stayed at the status “to be validated before development” — never launched, never invoiced.

Not a dramatic blocker: just a project waiting its turn, like dozens of others. Except that with Claude Code, “waiting its turn” no longer has quite the same opportunity cost.

4. Framing the gap precisely

Before building anything, we had to clearly separate what had already been delivered (the import / matching, phase 1) from what remained to be done (the steering, phase 2, never ordered) — to rebuild only the missing part, without starting from scratch or duplicating the integrator's work.

To frame precisely what the Wiser tool actually exposed (and therefore what the dashboard had to display), we went about it differently from yet another scoping workshop: thanks to Claude's built-in browser — a recent capability, Claude can now open a page and look at it — we had it watch a Wiser presentation video and extract the features and the data actually available. Enough to turn a demo into a list of concrete needs, without going back to the provider.

The reflex that changes everything

The question is no longer “how much does it cost to have this developed?” but “what already exists, and what's the smallest piece I'm really missing?”. Framing the gap well is already half the savings.

5. The foundation: a price that makes sense

A price-alignment dashboard is only worth something if “align a price” writes to the right place. That's the whole point of having, in parallel, overhauled the Odoo pricing system around a single field, the source of truth, that drives quotes, sales and the Shopify sync. Without that foundation, an “Align” button would be wishful thinking; with it, it changes the price, everywhere, at once.

6. What we built (first version)

Rather than requesting another external quote, the app was built directly with Claude Code — a first version, already being deployed:

  • Sync of Odoo data to a dedicated local database.
  • ABC scoring of products, reusing a module already in place rather than redoing it.
  • A dashboard with clickable indicators and a product table groupable by brand / category, with the competitor columns alongside.
  • A detail record per competitor, with an “Align” button wired to the price-alignment assistant that already existed on the Odoo side — so one click that really writes the new price.
  • Excel export, standard Cobra brand identity, and deployment on our internal apps hub.

Nothing speculative: we built exactly the missing piece, leaning on everything that already existed (the Wiser data, the scoring, the alignment assistant, the overhauled pricing system).

7. Build vs buy: the real math

The spec talked about 3 to 4 weeks of development for the rules engine part alone. The first version was built in-house in a few days, a fraction of that time — and above all, keeping control: every iteration gets settled live, with no quoting back-and-forth.

The lesson isn't “never call an integrator again.” On the contrary: the integrator had delivered the hardest building block (the Wiser import / matching), and it's precisely because that base was solid that we could build fast on top of it. The real build vs buy math isn't “do everything yourself” — it's buy what's hard and standard, build what's specific and changing.

And after

This dashboard isn't an isolated object: it joins a small ecosystem of internal applications that's growing fast at Cobra (cash flow, purchasing, salesperson performance, reporting…), all built from the same mold — shared brand identity, shared Odoo connection, standardized deployment. That momentum deserves its own story: a dedicated guide is in the works.

Update · September 1, 2026

And after: the engine went live

The “first version” of this dashboard got its sequel in August 2026: a rules engine in Odoo reads competitor prices at every delivery, computes a target price (best valid competitor, bounded by a minimum margin), ran in observation mode, then was switched live family by family — six families and a few hundred prices realigned, the rest adjusting on its own.
