An agent that processes
supplier invoices

It reads the emails, extracts each invoice, attaches it to the right purchase order in Odoo, checks the prices, and pushes it for one-click validation in Teams. Hosted on a Scaleway instance, triggered every 15 minutes. The real gain isn't speed — it's a workflow that can no longer drift.

1. The real problem: not speed, discipline

Odoo has been in place at Cobra since February 2025. On paper, the accounting chain is crystal clear: sales order (SO) → purchase order (PO) → invoice → payment → SEPA batch. Each invoice should point to the order that triggered it — that's what enables the 3-way match (order / receipt / invoice) and therefore the automatic control of prices and quantities.

In real life, that chain was never applied properly. The accounts-payable staff in place never mastered the tool: invoices were entered “flat,” disconnected from their original order, payments rarely reconciled correctly. The result: an Odoo full of unreadable entries, and a chain impossible to read end to end.

The angle of this piece

A rushed human skips attaching the invoice to the order. Not out of stupidity — because it's the tedious step, and no alarm goes off when you forget it. Discipline doesn't hold over time. A coded rule does. That's the whole point of handing this to an agent: not to “go faster,” but to make the error impossible by construction.

2. The drift, in numbers

Before writing a single line, we measured the real state of the database (read-only, via XML-RPC). The numbers speak for themselves — out of 5,450 supplier invoices posted since February 2025:

In plain terms: ~€2M of supplier invoices entered off-chain, with no link to any order, and €600k of unpaid invoices dragging on for more than two months. That's the cost of the drift — and precisely what the agent prevents from happening again.

An honest nuance, because this site is build in public, not brochure mode: the drift is mostly historical. Among the invoices detached from an order, 1,379 are old (> 2 months) versus 145 recent. It improved over time — but 145 recent ones prove that without an automatic constraint, human entry drifts again, always.

Making the chain readable: the 3 statuses on the order

First building block, on the Odoo side: I added three fields on the purchase order (purchase.order) to expose the complete state directly from the PO — before, you had to dig through invoices and payments separately.

FieldMeaningOn confirmed POs
DeliveredGoods received?complete 3,439 · partial 77 · no 309
InvoicedInvoice received and linked?complete 3,319 · partial 144 · no 362
PaidSettled?complete 3,003 · partial 147 · no 675

One glance at an order: delivered? invoiced? paid? The chain becomes readable again — which was impossible with invoices detached from their order.

Why the paid status is vital here

Cobra has almost no outstanding supplier credit: most suppliers are paid on receipt or on short terms. Knowing at any moment what is invoiced and paid, order by order, is therefore not accounting comfort — it's cash-flow steering. An invoice detached from its order is a payment you risk forgetting… or paying twice. That's exactly what these statuses, and their automatic tracking, make impossible.

3. What the agent does (and why it can't drift)

The agent doesn't “help” you enter data better. It replaces data entry with a workflow it is incapable of not following. The journey of an invoice, end to end:

  1. Invoice collection (two sources). Every 15 minutes, the agent picks up invoices where they arrive: the PDFs already attached to orders in Odoo (source A), and above all the mailboxes where suppliers converge — including a catch-all — through the Microsoft Graph API (sources B/C). It spots the attachments and email bodies that are invoices, and deduplicates (SHA-256 + message ID).
  2. Extraction. Depending on the supplier, each invoice is read either by a dedicated parser (known, structured formats, highly reliable), or — the general case — by OCR + the Claude API (Opus 4.8 model). In both cases, the output is a clean structure, with a confidence score: supplier, number, date, lines, amounts, VAT, WEEE eco-fee, shipping.
  3. Line-by-line matching. Each line is matched to the exact order line (identification hierarchy: supplier code → EAN → name → price), handling the silent traps (pair/unit quantities, WEEE fee and shipping bundled in or on a separate line).
  4. Blocking checks. Duplicate? Price gap > 5%? Order not found? → the invoice doesn't go to draft: it's parked for human follow-up (the “intervention” channel, or “to sort” if there's no readable order reference in the document).
  5. Human validation in Teams. A card (Adaptive Card) summarizes the invoice and offers one-click actions. The human never touches the Odoo interface again, except for the weekly SEPA batch.

The four guardrails that make drift structurally impossible:

  • Attachment guaranteed by construction. Each invoice line created points to the exact order line (purchase_line_id) and fills in the origin automatically. The agent doesn't know how to create a “free” invoice. 100% of what it creates is linked to its order — versus the 28% detached in the human-entered history.
  • Anti-duplicate. It refuses to create if the supplier reference already exists, or if the order line is already invoiced.
  • Price consistency. Invoice vs. order gap > 5% → block and request a corrected invoice from the supplier, before any write.
  • Constancy. The same rules, every 15 minutes, on every invoice, indefinitely. Code doesn't get tired, never skips the tedious step.

The price-gap check, in code

The “price consistency” guardrail isn't wishful thinking: it's a coded rule, which blocks before any write to Odoo. Beyond a 5% gap between the invoiced price and the price negotiated on the order, the agent creates nothing — the invoice is parked and flagged.

TOLERANCE_PRIX = 0.05   # 5%: beyond that, we create NOTHING, the invoice goes to intervention


def controle_ecart_prix(ligne_facture, ligne_commande):
    """Compare the invoiced unit price to the price negotiated on the purchase order (PO).
    ≤ 5%  : minor gap (bundled shipping, rounding) → the invoice follows the normal flow.
    > 5%  : blocking → no draft is created, the invoice is parked and flagged.
             The human then requests a corrected invoice from the supplier.
    """
    prix_po = ligne_commande.prix_unitaire_ht
    if not prix_po:                  # line without an order reference: can't be checked here
        return Verdict(statut="non_controle")

    ecart = (ligne_facture.prix_unitaire_ht - prix_po) / prix_po

    if abs(ecart) <= TOLERANCE_PRIX:
        return Verdict(statut="ok", ecart_pct=round(ecart * 100, 1))

    return Verdict(
        statut="bloquant",
        motif="ecart_prix",
        ecart_pct=round(ecart * 100, 1),
        message=(
            f"Invoiced price {ligne_facture.prix_unitaire_ht:.2f} € "
            f"vs order {prix_po:.2f} € ({ecart:+.1%}) — "
            f"beyond the {TOLERANCE_PRIX:.0%} tolerance"
        ),
    )

A bloquant (blocking) verdict goes up to the decision engine, which refuses to create the draft and routes the invoice to the “intervention” channel with its reason. That's anti-drift in one function: no human judgment call on every invoice, a rule that applies on its own, always.

4. The production architecture on Scaleway

The point that changes everything compared to the design phase: the agent exists outside my computer. It runs on a Scaleway instance (BASIC3-X2C-4G VM: 2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM, Paris region), for ~€36/month. French hosting — that matters for accounting data.

  • The batch: a cron every 15 minutes launches the agent loop (read emails → extraction → matching → Teams cards).
  • The endpoint: a small Flask server, for its part, runs permanently (systemd service) to receive the clicks on the Teams card buttons and write back to Odoo. It's reachable through a dedicated subdomain (agent-factures.cobra.fr, DNS at Gandi) rather than a bare IP, with an HMAC signature on every incoming request.
  • Reading emails: through Microsoft Graph (Microsoft 365 API, application flow), no IMAP. Reading emails directly at the source is what makes the agent autonomous: no human intervention to “forward it” an invoice.
  • Odoo: Odoo 18 Enterprise, XML-RPC access + API key.

Out of these 9 invoices: 3 validated and moved to an Odoo draft linked to their order, 1 already booked (payment mirror card), 3 parked in intervention (price gap, duplicate, order not found), 1 to sort (no order reference) and 1 rejected (delivery note, not an invoice). A note on the channels: “To validate” is the human action queue — it groups both the clean drafts ready to validate in one click and the parked cases to handle (price gap, duplicate…). The “To pay” and “Paid” channels, for their part, track the payment state on the Odoo side.

5. The numbers: volume, time, ROI

Measured volume: ~349 supplier invoices/month, i.e. ~80/week.

The human time per invoice (import, line-by-line check, reconciliation) is in the range of 7 to 12 minutes with manual entry. Taking a median of 9 minutes over 80 invoices/week:

Human time / week
Before the agent (entry + check + reconciliation)~12 h (≈ 0.35 FTE)
With the agent (validating the Teams cards + intervention cases)~2 to 2.5 h
Net time saved~9 to 10 h / week

Honesty: measured volume, estimated minutes

The volume (80/week) is measured in Odoo. The minutes per invoice are a market range for manual accounts-payable entry — not a stopwatch measurement. To be adjusted with our real times. On the cost side: this task occupied a full-time position in accounts payable, of which only about ten hours were actually productive for lack of mastery of the tool; on top of that came “extra” time taken from other people and an external accounting firm. At ~€3k/month fully loaded for an equivalent position (market estimate), the agent pays back its hosting (~€36/month) several dozen times over.

6. What the agent does NOT do

To stay credible, the scope has to be clear. The agent guarantees the invoice → order attachment and price consistency. It does not do:

  • Reconciliation / bank matching. Payment and the SEPA batch remain a human and banking step. What the agent brings on the payment side is visibility, not execution: three Teams sub-channels (To validate / To pay / Paid) reflect the Odoo payment state. But by delivering invoices that are clean and linked to their order, it makes downstream reconciliation trivial and reliable — where a detached invoice makes it manual and fragile.
  • Updating Teams cards “in place.” At every state change, the agent posts a new mirror card in the right channel — it doesn't modify the existing card (a limitation of Power Automate webhooks). A true live update through a Teams bot is identified as the next step, not done yet.
  • Cleaning up the past. The ~1,500 detached invoices and the 241 lingering unpaid ones remain a regularization project. The agent doesn't repair the history — it guarantees the future no longer gets dirty.

Point to harden (said honestly)

The secrets (API keys, Odoo access) currently live in a protected .env file on the instance, not in a dedicated secrets manager. It works, it's acceptable for a single-operator start — but it's the first hardening item on the list.

7. What this illustrates

The lesson isn't “AI processes invoices.” It's: when a business process drifts because it depends on human discipline, the right reflex isn't more training or more control — it's coding the rule so it applies on its own, always, without exception.

The agent isn't “faster than an accountant.” It is incapable of drifting. That's a different property, and it's the one that durably repairs an accounting chain, where no amount of human effort managed to.

Coming up / what's next

  • Live update of Teams cards (Teams bot rather than webhook) for a card that updates in place.
  • Secrets management through a dedicated vault.
  • Regularization project for the history (detached invoices, lingering unpaid) — semi-automatable once the incoming flow is under control.
  • Real measurement of before/after times to replace the market range with our own numbers.
