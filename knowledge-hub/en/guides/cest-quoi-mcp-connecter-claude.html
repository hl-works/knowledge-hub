What's an “MCP,” and how to plug Claude into your tools

API, MCP, browser, copy-paste: the four ways to plug Claude into your tools (Shopify, Gmail, Drive…), from the simplest to the most powerful. Why MCP changed everything — and, since 2026, why Claude no longer just reads: it acts, under your validation.

By Hugo Lahutte · May 31, 2026 · Updated September 1, 2026 · ~6 min read

30-second take

  • By default, Claude can't see your tools. It reasons, but touches nothing until you plug it in.
  • Four ways to connect it, from the simplest to the beefiest: copy-paste / screenshot → browser → API → MCP. In the Claude apps, a connected MCP is called a connector.
  • MCP is a standard socket that lets Claude read and act in Shopify, Gmail, Drive, GitHub… It turns the assistant into a colleague plugged into your business — hence one rule: it never has more permissions than you, and the actions that matter get validated.

1. The starting point: a head without arms

Claude on its own is a brilliant brain with no hands. It can analyze what you show it, but it knows nothing about your Shopify or your email until you open the door. “Connecting” Claude means giving it arms.

2. The 4 levels of connection

From the simplest to the most powerful:

  1. Copy-paste / screenshot — zero setup. You paste a text, a photo of a screen, an export. Perfect to get started.
  2. The browser — you let Claude drive a browser to go click and read on a site for you. Cowork has had one built in since August 2026, in its side panel.
  3. The API — a programmed pipe between a service and Claude. More technical, but automatable.
  4. MCP — the standard socket (see below). The cleanest and most durable connection.

3. What an MCP is, concretely

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It's an open standard created by Anthropic in late 2024 and adopted since by the whole market: think of a universal socket, like USB. Any MCP-compatible tool plugs into Claude — or into another assistant — with no custom tinkering. You “connect” Shopify, Google Drive, Gmail… and Claude can then read inside them and act, within the limits you allow.

The point: instead of re-coding an integration for each tool, everyone speaks the same socket. In 2026, vendors publish their own socket: Shopify for stores, Meta for ad accounts, hosting providers, online banks, invoicing tools. In the Claude apps, it's simply called a connector, switched on from a menu.

4. Read, then act: what changed in 2026

In the spring, connectors were mostly for reading: cross-checking sales, finding a document, summarizing an inbox. Since the summer, Claude acts: it drafts and sends a reply in Gmail, files or creates a document in Drive, updates a product page, opens a change request on a code repository — with control over the actions that require your validation.

It's a change in kind, and it imposes two rules I don't negotiate:

  • The assistant never has more permissions than the person talking to it. A connector inherits your account, not an admin account. On my side, each Claude has its own connectors: the Cobra Claude sees Odoo and the Cobra store, the HL Claude sees my projects and my Brain — never the other way around.
  • Automatic reads, validated writes. Reading and proposing: automatic. Sending, editing, publishing: a preview, then a button. The details of these regimes are in AI proposes, the human validates.

One more reason to be strict: an agent that reads outside content (a web page, an email) can run into booby-trapped instructions there — what's called prompt injection. The 2026 models resist it far better (Anthropic says it has nearly neutralized the problem with Opus 5 and Claude Code's Auto mode), but limited permissions remain the real protection: an assistant that can't wire money can't be manipulated into doing it.

5. What's connected on my side

Concretely: my two Shopify stores (Cobra and Haute Fidélité) live, my Drive and my email, GitHub — which holds my code, this site and my Brain —, the online bank for invoices and statements, the host of my apps to read logs and redeploy, and Odoo through code. The result: Claude works on my real tools, not a demo. That's the difference between “a nice chatbot” and “an operational colleague.”

Two examples from a typical week: cross-checking the store's orders against Odoo's all-channel sales for one brand, in a single question; or, from my phone, asking Claude Code to fix a page on this site and seeing it live a minute later.

6. MCP or skill? The arms and the know-how

The two get mixed up all the time. An MCP gives access to a tool: it's an arm. A skill describes a procedure: it's know-how. Publishing an entry on this site takes both — the GitHub connector to write, and the skill that knows in what order to update the twenty files. An arm without a procedure does anything, fast; a procedure without an arm stays a document.
