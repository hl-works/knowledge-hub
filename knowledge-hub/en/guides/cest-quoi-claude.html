What is Claude,
and why did I build everything on it?

Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, explained simply: who makes it, what the lineup looks like in September 2026 (the Claude 5 family), the products behind the name, why it's at the top of the pack — and why I made it the foundation of all my projects, for real, not in a demo.

30-second take

  • Claude is the AI assistant developed by Anthropic, an American lab that has made reliability and safety its obsession.
  • Since summer 2026, it's the Claude 5 family: Fable 5.1 (the most capable, released September 1), Opus 5 (the everyday high end), Sonnet 5 (the balanced one), and Haiku (the fast one). Four tiers, one rule: the cheapest one that gets the job done.
  • Behind the name, several products — Chat, Cowork, Code — and, in my setup, a single foundation: Cobra, Haute Fidélité, the daily briefs, this site, my Brain. Everything runs through Claude.

1. What Claude is, and who makes it

Claude is Anthropic's AI — a lab founded by former OpenAI researchers, with a clear guiding principle: models that are as reliable and predictable as possible, built for serious work rather than flashy performance.

In practice, Claude isn't one model but a family, available in sizes you pick based on what you need. The version name changes every few months — what stays constant is the "very powerful / powerful / balanced / fast" logic.

2. The lineup in September 2026: the Claude 5 family

A dated reference point, because everything moved this summer. In three months, Anthropic renewed the entire lineup:

  • Claude Sonnet 5 (June 30) — "the most autonomous Sonnet yet": it plans, and uses the browser and the terminal on its own. Its launch price ($2 / $10 per million tokens in / out) became permanent in August. It's the workhorse of agents.
  • Claude Opus 5 (July 24) — gets close to Fable 5's level for half the price ($5 / $25), and is presented as the model most resistant to prompt injection — an important point as soon as an agent reads the web or emails.
  • Claude Fable 5.1 (September 1) — successor to Fable 5 (released June 9), same tier, same price ($10 / $50), a one-million-token context window, cheaper cache reads, and refined cybersecurity guardrails (fewer false refusals). Its version without certain guardrails, Mythos 5.1, remains reserved for trusted-access programs. I told the whole story — including the June episode when the US government suspended Fable 5 for three weeks — in Fable, Mythos, Glasswing.
  • Claude Haiku 4.5 — still the fast one and the cheapest ($1 / $5), for volume.

What it changes for the user: the rule doesn't move — the right model is the cheapest one that gets the job done — but "the job" has become much longer and more autonomous. A Sonnet 5 does today what an Opus did in the spring. The details on prices and plans are in How much does AI cost.

3. The products behind the name

"Claude" is also several ways of using it, which I detail in Chat, Cowork, or Code:

  • Claude (Chat) — the conversation, on the web, in the app, and on mobile, with memory, projects, and connectors (Gmail, Drive, Shopify…).
  • Claude Cowork — the desktop assistant that carries out a job end to end, without a terminal: files, spreadsheets, a built-in browser, scheduled routines. On desktop, web, and mobile.
  • Claude Code — the agent that acts inside a project: reads, edits, runs, deploys. Terminal, web, desktop app, mobile.
  • And around them: Claude Design (mockups), Claude Science (research), Claude in Slack (a team member you mention), the API for developers, and hosted agents that run at Anthropic on a schedule.

Every paid plan includes Chat, Cowork, and Code. What sets the plans apart is usage volume, not access to the tools.

4. Why it's one of the champions right now

No BS: no model is "the best at everything," the right choice depends on the task, and the competition is fierce — OpenAI, Google, xAI, and the Chinese open models ship a major version every month. But a few objective signals put Claude at the top of the pack:

  • It powers the pros' coding tools. The AI editors developers use most rely on Claude; Anthropic even said in June that more than 80% of the code merged into its own codebase is now written by Claude.
  • It attracts the best. In May 2026, Andrej Karpathy — co-founder of OpenAI, former head of AI at Tesla, one of the field's greatest teachers — joined Anthropic. When someone of that caliber picks a home, it says a lot.
  • It's built for serious work. Writing, analysis, reasoning, code, and — new this year — resistance to manipulation when an agent reads outside content. That's exactly what a business needs.
  • The know-how spreads. Free training (Claude Academy, August 2026), open documentation, a connection standard (MCP) adopted by the whole market.

5. Why I, of all people, built everything on it

The real proof isn't a benchmark, it's what runs in my setup. I'm not a developer — strategy, marketing, purchasing, that's my trade — and yet, with Claude:

  • Cobra runs on management modules I framed and had written (purchasing, stock imports, shop sync, pricing system), an internal app hub, an engine that aligns prices against the competition, an agent that processes supplier invoices, and since September Growth, the action queue that replaces ten dashboards.
  • Haute Fidélité has its press ERP (HF-OS, built in five days), its online store, and its logistics set up the same way.
  • Two daily briefs land every morning — global hi-fi and AI news — written automatically from dozens of sources.
  • This site, bilingual, is written and published with Claude. And my Brain, the memory of all this, is read by every one of my Claudes.

The common thread: a non-technical person shipping to production things that used to take a team. That's the click.
