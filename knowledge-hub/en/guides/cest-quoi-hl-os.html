What is HL OS:
an operating system for running a group with AI

A store, a magazine, a brand, a personal venture — and ten tools that don't talk to each other. HL OS is the name I give to what I've been building since June 2026 to run all of it with AI: a shared foundation, five building blocks, and one rule — the AI proposes, the human validates. The map, without jargon.

By Hugo Lahutte · September 1, 2026 · ~8 min read

In 30 seconds

  • HL OS = a shared foundation (a database, an AI layer, identity and permissions, an action log) + five building blocks that plug into it: Zéro (mail and tasks), Knowledge (the Brain), Content Machine (publish), Growth (decide), AI Core — Socle IA — (write, search, secure).
  • One core, several contexts: Cobra and Haute Fidélité share the engines, never the accounts, the data or the rules.
  • Every action follows the same flow: request → analysis → preview → validation → execution → log → measurement. Reading is automatic; writing is validated.

1. Why an “OS”

My daily life is Odoo for sales, margin and stock; Shopify for the stores; Google Analytics, Ads, Search Console and Merchant Center for visibility; Brevo for email; the social networks; one mailbox per organization; a magazine to produce. Every tool knows how to produce its own dashboard. None of them answers, on its own, the question that matters: what deserves my time today?

Until 2025, the answer was “hire, or buy a suite.” In 2026, AI changes the scale of what a business owner who knows what they want can have built. So I stopped stacking tools and started building the layer above — the one that reads every tool, reconciles the facts, proposes actions and keeps the record. An operating system, in the literal sense: what everything else runs on.

The word is a bit big for an organization my size. I own it: the day I started calling this an OS, I stopped thinking of it as a collection of scripts.

2. The foundation

Everything rests on four shared things:

  • A shared database, inherited from HF-OS, the publishing ERP I built in five days in June: a single application (a headless CMS + a web framework + PostgreSQL) that serves the dashboard, the admin and the API. The building blocks don't reinvent storage: they plug into it.
  • A shared AI layer — the AI Core (Socle IA): writing in my voice (several voices, actually: the magazine, me in the first person, the travel journal), embeddings for search, a router that picks the model by tier (small, large, fallback) and logs every call, and a pattern of secrets encrypted server-side — never in the code, never in the Brain.
  • Identity and permissions: Google sign-in restricted to a whitelist, roles, and the rule that governs everything — the assistant never gets more rights than the person talking to it.
  • An action log: every write leaves a usable trace, idempotent whenever the connector allows it.

3. The five building blocks

A building block is a perimeter, a living specification, a development plan with success criteria (“we'll know it's good when…”) and a dated Decisions section. The five:

Growth — from ten dashboards to a queue of actions

The most advanced building block. It automatically pulls the facts available (Odoo, Shopify, Analytics, Ads, Search Console, Merchant Center, web health checks, email, social), reconciles them, and proposes a handful of actions ranked by impact, confidence, effort and risk. A short home page, a single bar to ask a question or prepare an action, and the expert views behind it, as evidence. A missing data point shows up as exactly that — never a silent zero. Details in the journal entry.

Content Machine — one capture, N destinations

It doesn't produce content, it carries a presence: a few photos, a voice memo, at a place, at a time, by an identified person — and the machine proposes the renderings (post, article, print page) to the right destinations, without ever publishing on its own. Dedicated guide: Content Machine.

Knowledge — the Brain

The durable memory, in the OKF format, read by all my AIs. Dedicated guide: What is a Brain.

4. One core, several contexts

The most important architecture decision, made from the start: Cobra and Haute Fidélité share the intelligence, never the accounts, the secrets, the data or the operating rules. The first Growth instance runs for Cobra; a Haute Fidélité instance will reuse the same core with its own accounts, data and goals — an isolated context inside HL OS. The Content Machine works the same way: a context (the provenance — where, when, why) is not a destination (where it publishes), and one context deploys to several destinations.

The benefit isn't only technical. It's what makes it possible to run a work account and a personal account, a Claude Cobra and a Claude HL, without ever mixing two perimeters in a base read from both sides.

5. The flow of an action

Whatever the building block, an action that touches the real world (a price, a stock level, a campaign, an email, a publication) follows the same path:

Requesta question, an intent
→auto
Analysisfacts, sources, freshness
→auto
Previewwhat's about to change
→human
Validationone button, one person
→auto
Executionthrough the connector
→auto
Log + measurementbefore / after, fed back in

Reading and refreshing can be automatic. Writing to Shopify, Odoo, an ad platform, the email tool or a social network stays behind required validation as long as no finer explicit permission exists. And a recommendation without a measurement after execution doesn't close the loop: it hasn't been “learned.”

6. Where it stands (September 2026)

Dated snapshot · September 1, 2026

  • Growth — in production for Cobra since September 1: action-first home, main bar, evidence views, reads from every connected source. Current priority: making audience measurement reliable before allowing any budget-shift recommendation. One particularity: this block was built with ChatGPT and Codex, with Claude involved only at the margins (the story and architecture).
  • Content Machine — v2 spec (July 30), foundation in place; the September 1 pivot unifies field capture, magazine repurposing and “publish this idea” into a single system. Next step: capture in under ten seconds, then a first Instagram rendering. Deadline: the Haute Fidélité show, late September.
  • Knowledge / Brain — in place since August 21; read through a GitHub connector; search index postponed until the signal.
  • Zéro and AI Core — in progress; the specifications are migrating into the Brain.

This status is a snapshot, not a lasting truth: figures and statuses live in the tools and in the Brain, never copied here as permanent.

7. What it illustrates

Three things I believe transfer to any small or mid-sized business:

  • The value isn't in one more dashboard. It's in no longer having to open ten interfaces to piece together a situation — and in having a queue of actions with their evidence.
  • The hard work wasn't the code. It was deciding which source to believe (Odoo carries the actuals, Shopify the web flow, the ad platforms their self-reported attribution) and preventing misleading reconciliations. The AI hammered out the code; the trade-offs stay human.
  • Build rather than wait for a quote, when the business is specific and the cost of customization collapses. The core of Growth was framed over a weekend and put into production on the Tuesday — built, for this block, with ChatGPT and Codex rather than Claude. The question “do you still need to buy?” now applies to the steering layer, not just the ERP.
