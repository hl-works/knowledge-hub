What is a "loop"? Loops, routines, and scheduled tasks

A classic agent works toward a goal and then stops. A loop is the level above: the same task re-run at a regular interval — monitor, check, alert — without you. Since summer 2026, there are three ways to do that, from the most ephemeral to the most durable. The concept, the three tools, and the framing.

30-second take

  • A loop = a prompt re-run at a regular interval by an agent (every 5 min, every hour…), to monitor or check something without you.
  • Three tools depending on how long you need it: the loop (ephemeral, inside your Claude Code session, expires on its own), the routine (scheduled, runs in the cloud on Claude's side, without your machine), the homemade scheduled task (cron, GitHub Actions, a server — for what has to run forever).
  • Simple rule: need it for a few hours → loop; recurring need that can live at Claude's → routine; production infrastructure → scheduled task. And always a frame: goal, budget, frequency, criterion.

1. The loop, the level above the agent

In What is an AI agent, I explained the assistant/agent difference: the assistant answers, the agent acts in a loop until the result — then stops. The loop adds the time dimension: the entire task is re-run at a regular interval. The agent no longer works once toward a goal, it comes back to check — again and again.

It's the natural pattern for everything that is monitoring: you never "finish" monitoring a deployment or an order flow; you look, you compare with the expected state, you alert if it drifts, and you come back later.

2. The loop in Claude Code

Claude Code (see Chat, Cowork, or Code) ships with a /loop command: you give it a prompt and an interval, and it re-runs that prompt at every due time, inside your session. Without an interval, it sets the pace itself based on what it's waiting for — an eight-minute deployment deserves one check, not eight.

The detail that made me adopt it: the guardrail is built in. A loop expires automatically after a few days — it runs one last time, then deletes itself. A forgotten loop can't run indefinitely or silently burn a budget. It's exactly the kind of frame I demand for any agent (goal, budget, frequency, success criterion) — except here, the boundary is set by the tool itself.

3. Loop, routine, or scheduled task?

All three re-run a task at a regular interval; what differs is the lifespan and where it runs.

In my setup, all three coexist. My two daily briefs (hi-fi and AI) are scheduled tasks: they have to run every morning, forever, with their secrets on a server. The seven-day watch on my audience measurement — comparing each morning the real orders, the web flow, and the analytics, read-only, without changing anything — is a routine. Checking that an Odoo deployment goes well this afternoon is a loop: useful for three hours, forgettable afterwards.

4. What it's good for, for a merchant

Everything that gets checked at a regular interval:

  • Orders: check every 30 minutes that the morning's orders are going out to fulfillment, alert if a status is stuck.
  • Production release: after a deployment, check every 10 minutes that the key pages respond and that nothing breaks.
  • Supplier feeds: monitor that an expected stock import has arrived and integrated properly.
  • Prices: my alignment engine recalculates target prices with each new delivery of competitor prices, several times a day — in observation mode first, then live, family by family.
  • Sources: my daily brief alerts me when a source has stopped responding for three days. Monitoring of the monitoring.

One cross-cutting rule, whatever the form: a recurring task reads and alerts; it doesn't write on its own into a production tool without a human having validated the action. Details in AI proposes, the human validates.
