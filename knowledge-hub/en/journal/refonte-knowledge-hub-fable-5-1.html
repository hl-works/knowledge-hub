Fable 5.1 revisits the whole site: method v2, six guides, a glossary doubled

Claude · HL · September 1, 2026 · Perso
metadocseo-gtmen-prodPerso

Context

This site was written in May and June 2026. Five months later, it was still right — and yet wrong: the method described instruction files pasted into a chat when they now live in my repos; the Brain didn't exist; Cowork was a promise; the model lineup had been renewed three times; skills, routines and connectors that act appeared nowhere; and six guides were missing to explain what I actually do today. A method site that's five months behind the method is the textbook case of the stale brain.

The day Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1, I handed it the full pass — the way I'd handed Fable 5, the day after its release in June, an audit of the same site. The mandate: reread everything, bring everything up to today's date, explain simply, keep it evergreen, invent nothing, and keep the glossary current.

What was done

  • The Brain first. The session started by reading my Brain (entities, building blocks, method, notes, sessions) to find out what had changed — not the model's memory, the source of truth. Then the site's own AI Watch, which held the exact chronology of the summer (Fable 5, its suspension, Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Cowork everywhere, Fable 5.1), and a few web searches to check the facts that might have moved.
  • The Method rewritten (v2): two layers of context — the CLAUDE.md files (“how to work with me”) and the Brain (“what we know”) —, three doors (Chat, Cowork, Code), two agents, skills and connectors, the two-stage loop (rule + capture), the guardrails, an updated starting prompt, and a changelog since May.
  • Six new guides: the Brain and the OKF format; context; skills (and hooks, subagents, plugins); “AI proposes, the human validates”; HL OS; the Content Machine.
  • Twenty-three guides revisited: seven rewritten in depth (Claude and the Claude 5 family; Chat, Cowork or Code; cost, with September's orders of magnitude; loops, routines and scheduled tasks; MCP and the connectors that act; agents and their forms in 2026; the CLAUDE.md and what it isn't), the others updated with dated inserts — Fable's eventful summer, the signals of 2026, what three months of GEO taught, the follow-ups to the case studies.
  • The glossary: from 27 to 82 terms, in French and in English, with links to the guides and the entries where each notion comes to life. Brain, OKF, skill, hook, subagent, routine, context, compaction, human validation, HL OS, Growth, Content Machine, provenance, idempotent, embeddings…
  • The Guides page reorganized (the new ones, understand, equip, method, decide, my systems, case studies), the home page, the project pages and the About page brought up to today's date, and three journal entries for the summer's projects: the Brain, HL OS, the Content Machine.
  • The English mirror of everything that moved, the sitemap, the search indexes, the llms.txt file in both languages, the consistency check — the full linking, as for any publication.

What was hard / unexpected

Deciding what stays dated and what becomes evergreen. A guide that tells the story of June 9 must not be rewritten as if it had been written on September 1: it gets an “update” insert at the top, and its story stays. A foundational guide (agents, cost, MCP), on the contrary, must be rewritten with no trace of its old version, because a reader in December shouldn't have to do the subtraction.

Publishing without betraying what's private. The Brain contains pages marked private; HL OS and the Content Machine are described there with details that don't get out (accounts, amounts, internal addresses, decisions in progress). The rule held: we publish the principles, the architecture, the firm decisions and the dated state; never a sync. Going from the Brain to the site is an editorial act.

Not inventing. On several points (what actually runs in production, release dates, prices), the temptation was to write what “should” be true. Every time: check in the Brain, in the AI Watch or on the web, or hold back. A wrong guide is worse than a missing guide — the same rule as for the Brain.

Stack

Claude Fable 5.1 in Claude Code (cloud session), the Brain read through the GitHub connector, the site's AI Watch as the chronology, a few web searches for the facts that move, a small Python page generator in the session's sandbox to keep the chrome identical, the repo's scripts (sitemap, llms-full, feeds, consistency check), subagents for the English mirror. Zero dependencies added to the site: still HTML and CSS, a single stylesheet.

What it illustrates

A method site is a public brain: it goes stale in silence while the journal grows. The pass isn't an event, it's a ritual — and the next one already has a date. Second lesson: the useful work wasn't writing, it was deciding what to say and what to leave out, guide by guide. Fable wrote; I arbitrated. As with everything else.
